The approach

Why there are game mechanics at all

Because they are aimed at a specific problem, and because none of them involve cartoon avatars or points that mean nothing. Every mechanic in this course maps onto something the SAT actually scores.

The problem they solve

Your child answered all 54 reading questions and left nothing blank — but got 17 right. That means they currently cannot tell which questions they know and which they are guessing at. That single blind spot is worth more points than any content gap.

How the mechanics answer it

Before answering a practice question, they choose whether they are confident. Confident and correct counts double. Confident and wrong triggers a review of what went wrong. Guessing quietly stops being free — which is exactly the habit the real exam punishes.

What we deliberately avoid

No badges for turning up, no streak that breaks if they miss a day, no leaderboard against strangers. Sixteen-plus students preparing for a real deadline find those patronising, and the research on adult learners agrees.

Inside a lesson

What two hours actually looks like

Every lesson runs the same three-part shape. The structure stays fixed on purpose — a student who is currently guessing needs the lesson itself to be the predictable thing in the room.

0:00 – 0:30

Trap forensics

Real exam questions are taken apart before any are answered. The job is not to find the right answer — it is to work out why each wrong answer was designed to be tempting.

  • Spotting the word that makes an answer too strong or too narrow
  • Recognising a rephrased sentence versus a genuine trap
  • Reading the examiner's intention, not just the text
0:30 – 1:15

Speed lab

Short timed sets at genuine exam pace — 71 seconds a question in reading, 95 in maths. This is where the confidence choice lives, and where a run of correct answers builds a multiplier.

  • Timer calibrated to real exam speed, not an arbitrary one
  • A wrong answer stops the clock and asks what kind of mistake it was
  • Personal-best tracking, so the competition is with last week
1:15 – 2:00

Simulation and teach-back

A full section under exam conditions, then the part that does the most work: your child explains their reasoning back to the tutor, out loud, including on the questions they got right.

  • Builds the stamina a three-hour exam demands
  • Exposes right answers reached for wrong reasons
  • Every mistake written into a running error log
Honesty about the design

What we adapted, and what we threw out

This course is built on a gamification blueprint originally written for IELTS classes of twenty-plus students on a purpose-built platform. A good deal of it does not survive contact with one student, two hours, and a laptop. Here is exactly what carried over and what did not.

Original mechanicIn this courseReason
Predicted band score, updating live Kept — a running SAT score estimate with a range, shown against the target rather than a bare number The single most motivating thing you can show a student is the distance left to travel, stated honestly
Confidence wagers before answering Kept, and made central It targets this student's exact weakness — answering everything with no sense of which answers are sound
Skill tree with locked advanced nodes Kept — four branches: reading, grammar, maths reasoning, exam craft Stops us teaching transitions before sentence boundaries; the order genuinely matters
Tokens earned to unlock mock exams Kept — and the scarcity is real, not invented Only four practice tests match the current exam format. They genuinely cannot be spent casually
Focus mode that hides all game layers Kept By week 10 the exam interface should feel plain and familiar, with nothing decorative on screen
AI marking of essays against band criteria Dropped The SAT has had no essay since 2021. There is nothing for it to mark
Speaking debates and rebuttal rounds Dropped The SAT has no speaking component at all
Team relays, breakout pods, peer marking Dropped, replaced by personal bests and teach-back All of it needs a class of students. This is one-to-one tuition
Live class leaderboards and heatmaps Dropped Nobody to rank against, and ranking a struggling student against peers would do harm here anyway

How it is actually built

The original blueprint proposed live servers, databases and AI marking services. What your child will actually use is a set of single web pages that open in a browser — no account, no app to install, no login, and nothing sent anywhere. Their work stays on the device in front of them.

Guarding against burnout

Two hours is a long time to concentrate. Each lesson is broken into 25 to 30 minute bursts with a genuine break between them, and the game layer can be switched off entirely at any point. If your child asks to turn it off, that is a sign it is working, not failing.

The route

Twelve weeks, three checkpoints

Three full practice exams sit at weeks 4, 8 and 12. They are the honest measure of whether this is working — and each one is sat under real exam timing, split across that week's two lessons.

What we need from you

The part that decides the outcome

Three to five hours a week, at home

Forty-eight hours of lessons will not reach 950 on their own. Homework is the larger half of this plan, and it is the single biggest variable in where the final number lands.

  • Grammar exercises on whatever was taught that week — this is where the fastest gains are actually won
  • One reading passage, annotated rather than just answered
  • A vocabulary log — every unfamiliar word, written in the sentence it appeared in

You do not need to mark any of it or understand the content. What helps is protecting the time and asking to see the error log once a week.

Three things you should know now

  • The target is ambitious. 950 is roughly a 240-point rise. It is achievable precisely because the current misses are so basic — there is more ground to recover at this level than at a higher one. On lesson time alone, expect closer to 800 to 850.
  • Reading will look flat first. Grammar and algebra move quickly; reading comprehension typically shows little change until around week 8 and then moves. Checkpoint one may look disappointing on reading. That is expected, not a warning sign.
  • The practice exam was not an official one. It was a third-party mock, and it left out an entire maths topic worth roughly a third of the maths paper. We teach that topic in week 7 regardless, and checkpoint two will tell us more than the original result did.

One thing to arrange

The real exam is taken on a computer, in the official Bluebook app. Everything we practise on is paper. Two or three practice sittings in the app at home — it is free, and takes an evening — will remove the last surprise on exam day. We will tell you when the timing is right.
