Teacher sheet · week 2 · session B · Mathematics

Turning words into equations

Systems and inequalities, but the real target is translation — the point where the reading weakness starts costing maths marks.

Goal for this session

Convert a worded situation into an equation or inequality without losing a condition.

What you need open

Kaplan Advanced Prep for 1600 — ch 11 heart of algebra, PDF p. 260–272

Running order

0:00 Retrieval warm-upFive slope-meaning questions from last session.
0:15 Translation drillSentences to equations, one line at a time, no solving. "At least" versus "more than". "Twice as many A as B" — which way round. Fifteen minutes of nothing but setup.
0:30 Trap forensicsPhase 1.
0:55 BreakTen minutes.
1:05 Speed labPhase 2.
1:35 SimulationPhase 3.
1:50 Error log and homework

What to watch for

  • The "twice as many" reversal. If it appears twice, stop and drill it in isolation — it will otherwise recur every week to week 12.
  • Whether they read the final sentence of the question before setting up.

Errors to expect

  • Reversing the ratio in a translation.
  • Using > when the wording says "at least".
  • Solving the system correctly and then answering for the wrong variable.

Homework to set

  • Kaplan Advanced Prep for 1600 ch 11 remaining drills, PDF p. 260–272
  • Rewrite three missed questions as a plain-English sentence first, then as an equation
