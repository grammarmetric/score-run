Linear equations, and what slope means

Algebra is the one real strength on the diagnostic — 52%. This session is about converting it, and about finding out exactly which 48% is failing.

Goal for this session

Solve linear equations reliably, and read slope and intercept as meaning rather than as letters in a formula.

PPP — how the lesson page is structured

Present BriefingThe rule is taught and one worked example is walked through line by line, then a free question that costs nothing. No timer, no HP at risk. Rule as the student sees it: “Solve the equation, then read the question again. It often asks for something other than x.”
Practice Recon, then waves3 recon questions where they label why each wrong option is wrong, then 8 timed questions at 95s each. Scaffolded: hints, a scanner perk, and shields from streaks.
Produce Boss5 questions, no hints and no scaffolding, with HP carried over from practice. This is the independent-application stage — do not coach through it.

If they lose all HP the run ends and restarts from the briefing. That is deliberate: a failed run costs about four minutes and they will usually go again without being asked.

Running order

0:00 Retrieval warm-upFive quick equations, no calculator. Purely to see the working, not the answers.
0:15 Trap forensicsPhase 1. Maths traps are different from reading traps: the wrong answers here are the answer to a slightly different question.
0:45 Slope as meaningTake one linear model and ask what the slope means in the sentence, in words, with units. Repeat until it is automatic. This is what makes the word problems in week 3 possible.
0:55 BreakTen minutes.
1:05 Speed labPhase 2. 95 seconds a question — real maths pace.
1:35 SimulationPhase 3.
1:50 Error log and homeworkSeparate maths page in the log. Classify each miss: method, setup, or arithmetic. The distinction matters.

What to watch for

  • Which of the three failure types dominates. Method failures need reteaching; setup failures are a reading problem; arithmetic slips are a pace problem. All three look identical on a score report.
  • Whether they reach for the calculator on questions that do not need one.

Errors to expect

  • Sign errors when moving a term across the equals sign.
  • Solving for x when the question asked for 3x — the single most common avoidable maths error on this exam.
  • Reading a graph's intercept as the slope.
