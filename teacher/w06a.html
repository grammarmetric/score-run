Teacher sheet · week 6 · session A · Reading & writing

Apostrophes and pronouns

Possession, contraction, and making a pronoun point at exactly one thing.

Goal for this session

Distinguish its/it's and their/they're/there reliably, and spot a pronoun with no clear antecedent.

PPP — how the lesson page is structured

Present Warm-up + 3 teaching modulesOpens with the “Two jobs, one mark” analogy, then teaches: Its and it’s · Singular and plural possession · A pronoun must point at one noun. Each module carries concept cards, a right/wrong example pair, the rule, the trap the exam builds from it, and a worked example in numbered steps. Nothing is timed and nothing costs a life.
Practice Recon, then waves3 recon questions where they label why each wrong option is wrong, then 8 timed questions at 71s each. Scaffolded: hints, a scanner perk, and shields from streaks.
Produce Boss5 questions, no hints and no scaffolding, with HP carried over from practice. This is the independent-application stage — do not coach through it.

If they lose all HP the run ends and restarts from the briefing. That is deliberate: a failed run costs about four minutes and they will usually go again without being asked.

What to watch for

  • Plural apostrophes creeping in — the most common written error and the easiest to fix.
  • Ambiguous "it" being accepted because the meaning is guessable from context.

Errors to expect

  • Its versus it's under time pressure.
  • A pronoun agreeing with the nearest noun rather than the intended one.
  • Apostrophes added to plurals.

Homework to set

  • College Panda SAT Writing ch 17 and ch 14 exercises
  • One reading passage, annotated
  • Vocabulary log
