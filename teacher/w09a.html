Teacher sheet · week 9 · session A · Reading & writing

Joining ideas

Transitions are heavily tested and entirely learnable — the highest-return topic left in the reading and writing section.

Goal for this session

Read the relationship between two sentences before looking at any choice, then pick the transition that matches it.

PPP — how the lesson page is structured

Present Warm-up + 3 teaching modulesOpens with the “Name the turn before you take it” analogy, then teaches: The five relationships · Cover the choices first · The two most-tested traps. Each module carries concept cards, a right/wrong example pair, the rule, the trap the exam builds from it, and a worked example in numbered steps. Nothing is timed and nothing costs a life.
Practice Recon, then waves3 recon questions where they label why each wrong option is wrong, then 8 timed questions at 71s each. Scaffolded: hints, a scanner perk, and shields from streaks.
Produce Boss5 questions, no hints and no scaffolding, with HP carried over from practice. This is the independent-application stage — do not coach through it.

If they lose all HP the run ends and restarts from the briefing. That is deliberate: a failed run costs about four minutes and they will usually go again without being asked.

What to watch for

  • Choosing a transition because it sounds sophisticated rather than because it matches the logic.
  • Whether they can state the relationship before seeing choices — if not, the habit has not formed yet.

Errors to expect

  • "However" used where the ideas agree.
  • "Therefore" used where there is no causal link.
  • Missing that the second sentence is an example rather than a consequence.

Homework to set

  • College Panda SAT Writing ch 19 exercises, PDF p. 117–122
  • One reading passage, annotated
  • Vocabulary log
