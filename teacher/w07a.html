Tenses and misplaced descriptions

Keeping time consistent across a passage, and making sure a describing phrase is next to the thing it describes.

Goal for this session

Choose a verb form from the passage's existing timeline, and detect a dangling modifier by asking who is doing the action.

PPP — how the lesson page is structured

Present BriefingThe rule is taught and one worked example is walked through line by line, then a free question that costs nothing. No timer, no HP at risk. Rule as the student sees it: “Tense is settled by evidence elsewhere in the passage. A describing phrase must sit next to the thing it describes.”
Practice Recon, then waves3 recon questions where they label why each wrong option is wrong, then 8 timed questions at 71s each. Scaffolded: hints, a scanner perk, and shields from streaks.
Produce Boss5 questions, no hints and no scaffolding, with HP carried over from practice. This is the independent-application stage — do not coach through it.

If they lose all HP the run ends and restarts from the briefing. That is deliberate: a failed run costs about four minutes and they will usually go again without being asked.

What to watch for

  • Tense chosen by what sounds natural in speech rather than by the passage timeline.
  • Dangling modifiers being read as fine because the meaning is recoverable.

Errors to expect

  • Switching tense mid-passage.
  • Using the past perfect where the simple past is correct.
  • An opening phrase describing the wrong noun.
