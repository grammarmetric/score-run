Teacher sheet · week 3 · session A · Reading & writing

Where one sentence ends

The highest-yield grammar topic on the exam. Knowing where a sentence stops decides a large share of every conventions set.

Goal for this session

Test any string of words for whether it is a complete sentence, and join two of them legally.

What you need open

College Panda SAT Writing — ch 8 run-ons, PDF p. 35–45
College Panda SAT Writing — ch 9 fragments, PDF p. 46–50

Running order

0:15 The clause testOne rule, taught until automatic: does this have a subject and a verb, and can it stand alone? Then the four legal joins — full stop, semicolon, comma plus FANBOYS, and the dependent-clause comma.
0:35 Trap forensicsPhase 1.

1:05 Speed labPhase 2.

What to watch for

  • Comma splices being read as correct because they "sound like a pause". This is the single most common error at this level.
  • Whether they can spot that "however" is not a conjunction.

Errors to expect

  • Joining two complete sentences with a comma.
  • Treating a long phrase as a sentence because it is long.
  • Using a semicolon before a fragment.

Homework to set

  • College Panda SAT Writing ch 8 and ch 9 exercises in full, PDF p. 35–50
  • One reading passage, annotated
  • Vocabulary log
